================================================================================
MASTER'S THESIS PROJECT
================================================================================

Title: "Predictive Modeling of Tuberculosis Treatment Outcomes in Sub-Saharan 
        Africa: A Bayesian Statistical Approach"

Supervisor: Prof. Richard Jones, Department of Statistics
Co-Supervisor: Dr. Sarah Williams, Institute of Global Health
Status: Completed with Distinction
Thesis Grade: 92/100

Research Objectives:
- Develop Bayesian statistical models to predict TB treatment outcomes
- Integrate patient demographic, clinical, and socioeconomic factors
- Identify key drivers of treatment success and failure in low-resource settings

Methodology:
- Analyzed 2,500+ patient records from 12 Sub-Saharan African clinics
- Developed hierarchical Bayesian models
- Implemented MCMC algorithms for parameter estimation
- Conducted sensitivity and robustness analyses

Key Findings:
- Identified 7 critical factors associated with treatment outcomes
- Model achieved 81% predictive accuracy
- Demonstrated effectiveness of Bayesian approach over frequentist methods
- Generated policy recommendations adopted by WHO collaborating centers

Research Impact:
- Thesis findings presented at International Statistical Society Conference
- Invited to present at 2 international public health conferences
- Research supported by WHO and Gates Foundation
- Findings inform TB control programs in 3 African countries

Publications:
- Okonkwo, A., Jones, R., Williams, S. (2021). "Bayesian Prediction of TB 
  Treatment Outcomes in Sub-Saharan Africa." Statistical Modelling in Medicine 
  and Public Health, (in preparation for submission)
